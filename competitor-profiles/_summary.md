## Implication for Roman Skin

A 60-day guarantee is competitive for a premium direct-to-consumer skincare brand. It is more generous than the mainstream 30-day standard without reaching Maelove's 100 days or The Ordinary's exceptional 365-day policy.

Recommended controls:

- Start the 60-day window on delivery, not purchase.
- Cover opened products with at least 50% remaining.
- Require return authorization and shipment within 14 days.
- Limit coverage to direct RomanSkinCare.com purchases.
- Require complete sets and bundles.
- Exclude original and return shipping except for damaged, defective, or incorrect orders.
- Limit satisfaction returns to one per product per household and monitor misuse.

## Provisional economics

Model basis: 100 orders, $86.30 gross merchandise AOV, $30.61 average landed product cost, and estimated packaging, fulfillment, outbound shipping, and payment costs. Marketing, payroll, platform costs, return freight, and return-processing labor are omitted.

| Scenario | Refund rate | Net revenue | Contribution profit | Contribution margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low claims | 2% | $8,457 | $3,996 | 47.3% |
| Expected claims | 4% | $8,285 | $3,829 | 46.2% |
| High claims | 8% | $7,940 | $3,493 | 44.0% |

Compared with a hypothetical 2% baseline, a 4% refund rate costs approximately $168 per 100 orders and an 8% rate costs approximately $503. The guarantee would need roughly a 4% order lift to cover the move from 2% to 4% refunds, or roughly a 9% lift if the current refund rate is near zero and the new rate reaches 4%.

These are planning estimates, not a store forecast. Replace the assumed refund rate, AOV, variable costs, and conversion impact with Shopify and analytics actuals after the first 30–90 days.
